## 2. Picture description

### Barrel and materials

A large wooden barrel occupies the left side of the composition. Its curved body is built from warm brown vertical staves, with dark seams, rough grain and small surface cracks. Amber and pale orange highlights catch the forward-facing wood and the freshly exposed broken edges. The inside of the barrel is much darker, making the open cavity easy to read.

Exactly one broad iron hoop remains attached around the lower part of the barrel. The band follows the rounded body, with a dark gray, mottled surface, a narrow pale highlight along its upper edge and two clearly visible round rivet heads on its front. A separate loose iron hoop lies on the stone floor to the left of the barrel.

### Broken silhouette and scattered pieces

The barrel's upper half is shattered into irregular, upward-pointing staves. Some fragments lean outward, while other short boards rise from the rear edge of the opening. Long splinters and jagged breaks give the upper silhouette an uneven rhythm. The barrel is viewed at an angle that reveals both its curved front and its dark interior.

The detached hoop rests as a tilted ellipse on the ground in the lower-left area. Several small pieces of broken wood lie around it on the paving stones. Warm light picks out these fragments and the barrel's left-facing edges; there are no surrounding spell rings or magical particle trails. The main wooden form and its debris remain concentrated to the left of the quiet lower-right area.

### Background and card frame

A large stone dungeon arch spans the upper background, supported by shadowed columns on both sides. Charcoal, slate-blue and muted violet blocks frame a dark masonry wall. A small recessed arched shelf on the right contains dimly visible vessels. Uneven stone floor slabs extend below the barrel and down the lower-left side, then fade into the dark background.

A thin gold border follows the card perimeter, with small gold oval hoop ornaments at the top corners and star accents at the bottom corners. The background becomes smooth, very dark midnight blue toward the reserved text area. The lower-right star ornament remains beyond the reserved text rectangle, near the outside corner. There are no names, numbers, captions, logos or placeholder text in the image.

## 3. Reserved text rectangle

Coordinates use the **top-left image boundary as `(0, 0)`**, with x increasing to the right and y increasing downward. Coordinates are zero-based. The rectangle includes its left and top edges and excludes its right and bottom edges.

**Canonical rectangle: `(x: 450, y: 952, width: 490, height: 364)` pixels.**

| Measurement | Pixels | Fraction of the complete image |
|---|---:|---:|
| Left edge / x | 450 | 45% of width |

| Width | 490 | 49% of width |

## 4. Placement at other display sizes

Preserve the card's 5:7 aspect ratio and scale the image and text rectangle together. When the displayed card has width `W`, its height is `H = W × 1.4`. For an image drawn at screen position `(imageLeft, imageTop)`:

```text
scale = W / 1000
textX = imageLeft + 450 * scale
textY = imageTop  + 952 * scale
textWidth  = 490 * scale
textHeight = 364 * scale
```

These examples use `(x, y, width, height)`. If a UI container adds padding or letterboxing, calculate the rectangle from the actual displayed image origin and size, including that offset. Cropping the card changes the mapping and can remove part of the reserved area or its border.

For an engine that uses a **bottom-left origin with y increasing upward**, the same native rectangle is **`(450, 84, 490, 364)`**. The bottom offset is `1400 - 1316 = 84`. In normalized bottom-left coordinates, its rectangle is `(0.45, 0.06, 0.49, 0.26)`. Use one coordinate convention consistently.

## 5. Integration notes

- Draw the card first, then draw the game's text and UI elements over the reserved rectangle.
- Use light text, such as ivory or near-white, against the dark field. Choose the font size, line spacing and internal padding for the actual display size.
- Wrap and clip text to the reserved rectangle. The artwork does not define a particular title line, statistic layout or number of description lines.
- Keep the image upright and preserve its full composition. Mirroring would move the quiet text region to the opposite side and require a different overlay layout.
- Use nearest-neighbor/point sampling when retaining crisp pixel edges is the priority. Avoid lossy recompression or texture settings that introduce halos.
- Keep the gold border and corner accents visible. The lower-right ornament sits outside the reserved bounds and should not become part of the text layout.

The image contains artwork only. Overlay text, fonts, interaction behavior and any animated UI effects are supplied by the game.
