## 2. Picture description

### Skeleton and sash

The skeleton knight has a large, cracked tan-and-ivory skull, dark empty eye sockets and an open jaw. The rib cage, spine, pelvis, arms and legs are exposed, with small pits and fractures across the weathered bones. A dark-red sash is knotted around the waist, and long torn cloth strips hang down on the left side of the image. The figure wears no armor or helmet.

The skull sits in the upper-left portion of the card and faces left in a three-quarter view. The torso and pelvis occupy the left half of the composition. Both legs and bare skeletal feet remain visible below, with one knee bent outward and the other leg extending toward the lower edge. The character is the main focal element.

### Sword and stance

The skeleton grips one short sword in the raised hand on the left side of the image. The blade rises beside the skull toward the upper left. Its gray metal edge is visibly chipped and uneven, and a simple crossguard separates the blade from a dark wrapped grip. The sword is the figure's only held weapon.

The other hand is empty and open beside the torso, with the fingers curved downward. Bent knees and separated feet give the skeleton a braced stance on the stone floor. The sword and both hands stay above the reserved text region, while the sash, legs and feet stay to its left.

### Background and card frame

A broad stone dungeon arch spans the upper background, with thick side columns and shadowed slate-blue masonry. A smaller arched recess in the upper-right area contains upright spear shafts and tips beside a tattered dark-red hanging banner. Uneven stone floor blocks are visible beneath the feet in the lower left. The background becomes smooth, very dark midnight blue toward the reserved text area.

A thin antique-gold border follows the card perimeter. Small sword ornaments decorate the upper corners, while diagonal bone ornaments decorate the lower corners, with tiny star-like gold accents nearby. The lower-right corner ornament remains beyond the reserved text rectangle, near the outside corner. There are no names, numbers, captions, logos or placeholder text in the image.

## 3. Reserved text rectangle

Coordinates use the **top-left image boundary as `(0, 0)`**, with x increasing to the right and y increasing downward. Coordinates are zero-based. The rectangle includes its left and top edges and excludes its right and bottom edges.

**Canonical rectangle: `(x: 450, y: 952, width: 490, height: 364)` pixels.**

| Measurement | Pixels | Fraction of the complete image |
|---|---:|---:|
| Left edge / x | 450 | 45% of width |

## 4. Placement at other display sizes

Preserve the card's 5:7 aspect ratio and scale the image and text rectangle together. When the displayed card has width `W`, its height is `H = W × 1.4`. For an image drawn at screen position `(imageLeft, imageTop)`:

```text
scale = W / 1000
textX = imageLeft + 450 * scale
textY = imageTop  + 952 * scale
textWidth  = 490 * scale
textHeight = 364 * scale

In normalized bottom-left coordinates, its rectangle is `(0.45, 0.06, 0.49, 0.26)`. Use one coordinate convention consistently.

## 5. Integration notes

- Draw the card first, then draw the game's text and UI elements over the reserved rectangle.
- Use light text, such as ivory or near-white, against the dark field. Choose the font size, line spacing and internal padding for the actual display size.
- Wrap and clip text to the reserved rectangle. The artwork does not define a particular title line, statistic layout or number of description lines.
- Keep the image upright and preserve its full composition. Mirroring would move the quiet text region to the opposite side and require a different overlay layout.
- Use nearest-neighbor/point sampling when retaining crisp pixel edges is the priority. Avoid lossy recompression or texture settings that introduce halos.
- Keep the gold border and corner accents visible. The lower-right ornament sits outside the reserved bounds and should not become part of the text layout.

The image contains artwork only. Overlay text, fonts, interaction behavior and any animated UI effects are supplied by the game.
