## 2. Picture description

### Lever and materials

A rusted iron floor lever is bolted to a broad, chipped slate-gray stone base. Its narrow shaft ends in a thicker, worn brown grip with cool highlights along the upper surface. A large circular, faceted pivot bolt sits on the near side of a rounded triangular iron housing; the visible rear side gives the mechanism depth.

The rectangular mounting plate has worn edges, patches of muted rust and separate corner fasteners. A small solid stop block stands beneath the lowered arm near the housing. The stone plinth has a pale chipped top edge, cracks and dark side faces, making its weight and material clear.

### Pulled pose and composition

The handle extends almost horizontally to the left from the pivot, rising only slightly toward the grip. It reads as the fully pulled position. The grip projects beyond the base and remains separated from the stone by dark negative space, while the shaft, pivot, stop and mounting plate remain individually readable.

The mechanism is shown from slightly above in three-quarter view, exposing the mounting plate and the top and front of the stone base. It occupies the lower-left and middle-left foreground, resting on worn flagstones. The complete handle and base stay within the card border. The image depicts a mechanical interaction state and contains no spell effects.

### Background and card frame

A dim dungeon gate alcove fills the upper background. Dark vertical portcullis bars, pointed lower ends and riveted horizontal braces sit beneath a large slate arch. Cool navy shadows keep the gate secondary to the lever, while the floor detail fades toward the dark lower-right field.

A thin antique-gold border follows the perimeter. Small solid, faceted rivet-head motifs occupy the upper corners, and star-like accents sit near the lower corners. The lower-right ornament remains outside the reserved rectangle. There are no names, on/off markings, labels, numbers or logos.

## 3. Reserved text rectangle

Coordinates use the **top-left image boundary as `(0, 0)`**, with x increasing to the right and y increasing downward. Coordinates are zero-based. The rectangle includes its left and top edges and excludes its right and bottom edges.

**Canonical rectangle: `(x: 450, y: 952, width: 490, height: 364)` pixels.**

| Measurement | Pixels | Fraction of the complete image |
|---|---:|---:|

## 4. Placement at other display sizes

Preserve the card's 5:7 aspect ratio and scale the image and text rectangle together. When the displayed card has width `W`, its height is `H = W × 1.4`. For an image drawn at screen position `(imageLeft, imageTop)`:

```text
scale = W / 1000
textX = imageLeft + 450 * scale
textY = imageTop  + 952 * scale
textWidth  = 490 * scale
textHeight = 364 * scale
```

In normalized bottom-left coordinates, its rectangle is `(0.45, 0.06, 0.49, 0.26)`. Use one coordinate convention consistently.

## 5. Integration notes

- Draw the card first, then draw the game's text and UI elements over the reserved rectangle.
- Use light text, such as ivory or near-white, against the dark field. Choose the font size, line spacing and internal padding for the actual display size.
- Wrap and clip text to the reserved rectangle. The artwork does not define a particular title line, statistic layout or number of description lines.
- Keep the image upright and preserve its full composition. Mirroring would move the quiet text region to the opposite side and require a different overlay layout.
- Use nearest-neighbor/point sampling when retaining crisp pixel edges is the priority. Avoid lossy recompression or texture settings that introduce halos.
- Keep the gold border and corner accents visible. The lower-right ornament sits outside the reserved bounds and should not become part of the text layout.

The image contains artwork only. Overlay text, fonts, interaction behavior and any animated UI effects are supplied by the game.
